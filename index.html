Text description of the video

A screen recording of SlateMind. The student types a name and presses “Begin Your Journey”, then enters the topic “How the Internet Works”. The app writes the lesson on the green blackboard line by line: IP addresses locate devices, DNS translates domain names, TCP/IP manages data transmission, a formula for data rate, and a Packet Switching heading. A Pop Quiz panel then asks three questions, ending with “What is an IP address?”. The clip finishes on the Badges screen, which shows the collection of badges to unlock.

Three steps, about a minute

Most AI tools hand you a wall of text. SlateMind slows down and teaches the way a good teacher does at the board.

Type a topic

Anything from “how vaccines work” to “binary search”. Or take the daily challenge.

Watch it written

Check yourself

Answer a 3-question quiz, ask a follow-up, and earn XP, badges and streak days.

What’s in the app today

Everything below works right now. Nothing here is a mock-up.

  • Animated blackboardHeadings, bullets, formulas, circled key ideas and simple diagrams drawn as handwriting.
  • Auto-made quizThree questions after every lesson, each with a one-line explanation.
  • Ask a follow-upNot clear? Ask on the board and get a short answer written below the lesson.
  • Glossary and fun factKey terms and one surprising fact for every topic.
  • XP, levels, 16 badgesTen levels and daily streaks that reward showing up.
  • Daily challengeA new topic each day with bonus XP.
  • Save as an imageExport any lesson board as a PNG for your notes.
  • Your pace, your lookSpeed control, sound on or off, and three chalk colour themes.

Your data, plainly

This page sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads no third-party fonts or scripts.

In the app, the topic you type is sent to our server and to Groq, the AI provider that writes the lesson. We don’t ask for your name, email or any account to use it. Your XP, streaks and history are stored only in your own browser, and “Reset all” in settings clears them.

SlateMind is meant for learners of all ages. Before accounts or payments exist, we will add a parental consent step for anyone under 18, as India’s data protection rules require. Read the full Privacy Policy.

Who makes this

SlateMind is made by Saksham Mogha, a Class 12 computer science student and independent developer in Faridabad, India. It began as a school Skill Expo project and the code is open source under the Apache 2.0 licence.
